Choosing a Method

This page routes a research question to the estimator that answers it. Find the row that matches your data and goal, then follow the page link for the model specification, keyword arguments, and worked examples. If you have not estimated anything with the package yet, run through Installation & First Model first — it takes ten minutes and establishes the workflow every section below assumes.

The first question is what your data looks like. Everything else follows from it.

Every estimator accepts a TimeSeriesData, PanelData, or CrossSectionData container directly, so the data step is the same regardless of which row you land on.

Univariate Time Series

One series observed over time. The choice turns on whether you want to remove a component, model the conditional mean, model the conditional variance, or let the data choose the functional form.

Fit an ARMA/ARIMA model and forecastestimate_arima, auto_arima, estimate_sarimaAutomatic order selectionARIMA
Model long memoryestimate_arfima, gph_testFractional differencing parameterARIMA
Extract an unobserved trend or cycle stateestimate_statespace, local_level, local_linear_trendKalman filter with estimated hyper-parametersState-Space Models
Let regression coefficients driftestimate_tvp_regRandom-walk coefficient pathsState-Space Models
Model time-varying volatilityestimate_garch, estimate_egarch, estimate_gjr_garch, estimate_svConditional variance dynamics and leverageVolatility Models
Model asymmetric or regime-dependent dynamicsestimate_threshold, estimate_setar, estimate_star, estimate_msPiecewise or smooth regime transitionsNonlinear Time Series
Estimate a shape without imposing onekernel_density, kernel_reg, lowessData-driven smoothing, no parametric formNonparametric Regression

Filters and state-space models both deliver a trend-cycle split. Use a filter when the decomposition is a preprocessing step; use a state-space model when the trend is an object of inference with standard errors.

Several series observed jointly. The dividing lines are stationarity, the ratio of parameters to observations, and whether you want the full system or a single dynamic response.

A mix of $I(0)$ and $I(1)$ regressorsestimate_ardl, bounds_test, long_runNo unit-root pre-testing requiredARDL & Bounds Testing
One efficient cointegrating vectorestimate_cointregEndogeneity-corrected long-run coefficientsCointegrating Regression
Dynamic responses robust to misspecificationestimate_lp, estimate_lp_iv, estimate_smooth_lp, estimate_state_lp, lp_fevdHorizon-by-horizon, no iterated biasLocal Projections

VAR and local projections answer the same question with different bias-variance trade-offs: the VAR is efficient when correctly specified, local projections are robust when it is not. Estimate both when the impulse responses drive the conclusion.

Structural Analysis and Identification

Reduced-form residuals are correlated across equations and carry no economic interpretation. Identification recovers the structural shocks; innovation accounting reports what they do.

Identify from changing volatilityidentify_markov_switching, identify_garch, identify_smooth_transition, identify_external_volatilityRegime variance shifts identify shocksHeteroskedasticity
Compare the statistical schemesirf(m, H; method=...)Eighteen method= symbols, one callStatistical Identification
Check that identification holdsindependence and non-Gaussianity testsStatistical ID needs testable assumptionsIdentification Testing
Trace a shock through timeirfDynamic causal effect pathsImpulse Responses
Attribute forecast error variancefevdRelative importance by horizonVariance Decomposition
Explain observed historical movementshistorical_decompositionShock contributions to actual dataHistorical Decomposition
See how the three fit togetherirf, fevd, historical_decompositionShared identification, three questionsInnovation Accounting

Sign, narrative, and long-run restrictions require economic assumptions you must defend. The statistical schemes require distributional assumptions instead — test them with the tools on Identification Testing before reporting the shocks.

Two-way fixed effects is biased under staggered adoption with heterogeneous effects. Start from estimate_did with a modern estimator, then use the Bacon decomposition and negative-weight diagnostics on Difference-in-Differences to show what TWFE would have missed.

Solve linearly first. Move to higher-order perturbation or a global method only when the question depends on curvature — risk pricing, large shocks, or a binding constraint.

Bridge equations aggregate the high-frequency indicator before regressing; MIDAS keeps every high-frequency observation and estimates the weights. Use the bridge as a baseline and MIDAS when release timing within the quarter carries information.
