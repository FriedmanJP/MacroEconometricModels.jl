State-Space Models

The state-space module provides a general, user-specified linear-Gaussian state-space object with maximum-likelihood estimation of its hyper-parameters, Kalman filtering, RTS smoothing, and forecasting. It is a thin, documented front end over the package's consolidated Kalman kernel — no separate filter is introduced — and covers the central-bank staples: unobserved-components trend/cycle models, structural time series (Harvey 1989), and time-varying-parameter (TVP) regression (Durbin & Koopman 2012).

The univariate siblings are ARIMA Models, whose exact MLE runs through the same kernel, and Volatility Models.

  • General system — any single-block observation/state pair via StateSpaceModel and estimate_statespace, with optional state-observation noise cross-covariance G.
  • Convenience wrappers — local_level, local_linear_trend, estimate_tvp_reg.
  • Steady-state & forecasting — generalized Schur Riccati solver via steady_state_kalman / SteadyStateKalman, standardized one-step residuals, filtered/smoothed states with variances, and multi-step forecast.

The linear-Gaussian state-space form

Every model in this module is a single-block linear-Gaussian system:

\[\begin{aligned} y_t &= Z \, \alpha_t + d + \varepsilon_t \\ \alpha_{t+1} &= T \, \alpha_t + c + R \, \eta_t, \qquad \operatorname{Cov}\begin{pmatrix} \eta_t \\ \varepsilon_t \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} Q & G \\ G' & H \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}\]

where:

  • $y_t$ is the $n_\text{obs} \times 1$ observation vector at time $t$
  • $\alpha_t$ is the $n_x \times 1$ unobserved latent state vector
  • $Z$ is the $n_\text{obs} \times n_x$ observation measurement matrix
  • $d$ is the $n_\text{obs} \times 1$ observation intercept vector
  • $\varepsilon_t \sim N(0, H)$ is the observation disturbance vector with $n_\text{obs} \times n_\text{obs}$ covariance $H$
  • $T$ is the $n_x \times n_x$ state transition matrix
  • $c$ is the $n_x \times 1$ state intercept vector
  • $R$ is the $n_x \times n_\eta$ state innovation selection matrix
  • $\eta_t \sim N(0, Q)$ is the $n_\eta \times 1$ state disturbance vector with $n_\eta \times n_\eta$ covariance $Q$
  • $G$ is the optional $n_\eta \times n_\text{obs}$ cross-covariance $\operatorname{Cov}(\eta_t, \varepsilon_t)$ (nothing when disturbances are uncorrelated)

Filtering and smoothing run through the shared kernel _kalman_filter! / _rts_smoother; the estimator maximizes the prediction-error-decomposition log-likelihood $\sum_t \log p(y_t \mid y_{1:t-1})$.

Initialization

The default is init_mode=:kappa: a large-$\kappa$ approximate-diffuse prior $P_1 = \kappa I$ with $\kappa = 10^6$ on every state direction, matching statsmodels' UnobservedComponents default. The alternatives are

init_mode$P_1$
:kappa (default)$\kappa I$ on all directions
:diffuse$\kappa$ on the nonstationary subspace, the stationary Lyapunov solution elsewhere
:stationarythe Lyapunov solution (errors if the transition is not stable)
:explicitwhatever a1/P1 you pass

:kappa and :diffuse coincide only when every direction is nonstationary — a pure local level, for instance — which is why the difference is easy to miss. :diffuse emits one warning per session whenever it finds a unit root and installs the $\kappa$ prior, and falls back to the stationary Lyapunov solution outright when the transition is stable.

`a1` and `P1` must be supplied together

Passing only one raises an ArgumentError, and passing both switches the model to init_mode=:explicit whatever else was requested. Before v0.7.2 the supplied half was silently discarded, so StateSpaceModel(Z, H, T, Q; a1=[999.0]) quietly returned the $\kappa$-initialized model instead of the one written. The two are not close: on a 150-observation random walk the $\kappa$ model scores a log-likelihood of $-248.6$ against $-190413$ for the explicit $a_1 = 999$, $P_1 = 1$ initialization — and the ignored specification is the plausible-looking one, so nothing prompted a second look (#512).

Local level

The local-level (random-walk-plus-noise) model decomposes a series into a stochastic level plus irregular noise:

\[\begin{aligned} y_t &= \mu_t + \varepsilon_t, \qquad \varepsilon_t \sim N(0, \sigma^2_\varepsilon) \\ \mu_{t+1} &= \mu_t + \eta_t, \qquad \eta_t \sim N(0, \sigma^2_\eta) \end{aligned}\]

where:

  • $y_t$ is the observed series at time $t$
  • $\mu_t$ is the unobserved stochastic level state
  • $\varepsilon_t \sim N(0, \sigma^2_\varepsilon)$ is the Gaussian observation noise with variance $\sigma^2_\varepsilon$
  • $\eta_t \sim N(0, \sigma^2_\eta)$ is the Gaussian level innovation with variance $\sigma^2_\eta$

local_level estimates $(\sigma^2_\varepsilon, \sigma^2_\eta)$ by MLE. The classic test case is the annual Nile river-flow series at Aswan, 1871–1970:

m = local_level(nile)
round.(m.theta, digits=1)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 15109.2
  1463.5

The irregular variance $\hat\sigma^2_\varepsilon = 15109.2$ and the level variance $\hat\sigma^2_\eta = 1463.5$ reproduce the Durbin & Koopman (2012, §2) benchmark values 15099 and 1469 to within 0.1% and 0.4% — the residual gap is the large-$\kappa$ prior standing in for exact diffuse initialization. The signal-to-noise ratio $\hat\sigma^2_\eta / \hat\sigma^2_\varepsilon = 0.097$ is small, so the smoothed level $\mu_{t|T}$ is a heavily damped version of the data: it tracks the well-known drop after the 1899 Aswan dam construction — the smoothed level falls from 1107 at the start of the sample to 799 at the end — without chasing high-frequency noise.

plot_result(m)   # observed vs filtered vs smoothed level with 68% band

Local linear trend

The local-linear-trend model adds a stochastic slope $\beta_t$ to the level, so the trend can drift in both level and growth rate:

\[\begin{aligned} y_t &= \mu_t + \varepsilon_t \\ \mu_{t+1} &= \mu_t + \beta_t + \xi_t \\ \beta_{t+1} &= \beta_t + \zeta_t \end{aligned}\]

where:

  • $y_t$ is the observed time series at time $t$
  • $\mu_t$ is the latent stochastic level component
  • $\beta_t$ is the latent stochastic slope (drift) component
  • $\varepsilon_t \sim N(0, \sigma^2_\varepsilon)$ is the observation measurement noise with variance $\sigma^2_\varepsilon$
  • $\xi_t \sim N(0, \sigma^2_\xi)$ is the level disturbance with variance $\sigma^2_\xi$
  • $\zeta_t \sim N(0, \sigma^2_\zeta)$ is the slope disturbance with variance $\sigma^2_\zeta$
  • $[\mu_t, \beta_t]'$ is the bivariate latent state vector
mt = local_linear_trend(nile)
report(mt)

  Observations: 100   State dim: 2   Obs dim: 1
  Log-likelihood: -646.1573   Converged: true   Init: :kappa

  σ²_ε   14676.7025        NaN   —       —            —             —
  σ²_ξ    1753.7795        NaN   —       —            —             —
  σ²_ζ     5.13e-10        NaN   —       —            —             —


  Std. residual Ljung–Box(10): Q = 13.278  p = 0.2085

On the Nile the MLE drives the slope variance to the boundary, $\hat\sigma^2_\zeta = 0$, with $\hat\sigma^2_\varepsilon = 14676.7$ and $\hat\sigma^2_\xi = 1753.8$. The data therefore prefer no stochastic slope at all: the fitted trend is a random walk with a fixed drift, which is the local level plus one redundant state. Do not read the reported log-likelihood ($-646.2$) as evidence against the local level's $-641.0$ — the trend model carries a second diffuse direction and so a second $-\tfrac12\log\kappa$ offset (Pitfall 1). Setting $\sigma^2_\zeta = 0$ by hand collapses the model to that smooth deterministic-slope trend; setting $\sigma^2_\xi = 0$ instead gives the integrated-random-walk (smooth-trend) model.

Time-varying-parameter regression

estimate_tvp_reg fits a regression whose coefficients follow a random walk:

\[\begin{aligned} y_t &= X_t \, \beta_t + \varepsilon_t, \qquad \varepsilon_t \sim N(0,\sigma^2_\varepsilon) \\ \beta_{t+1} &= \beta_t + \eta_t, \qquad \eta_t \sim N(0, \operatorname{diag}(\sigma^2_\eta)) \end{aligned}\]

where:

  • $y_t$ is the scalar observed dependent variable at time $t$
  • $X_t$ is the $1 \times k$ vector of regressors (including optional intercept) at time $t$
  • $\beta_t$ is the $k \times 1$ vector of time-varying regression coefficients (latent state)
  • $\varepsilon_t \sim N(0, \sigma^2_\varepsilon)$ is the observation error with variance $\sigma^2_\varepsilon$
  • $\eta_t \sim N(0, \operatorname{diag}(\sigma^2_\eta))$ is the $k \times 1$ vector of parameter innovations with diagonal covariance $\operatorname{diag}(\sigma^2_\eta)$

The observation matrix $Z_t = X_t$ is time-varying, so the coefficient path $\beta_t$ is recovered as the smoothed state. A leading intercept column is added by default (intercept=true), which puts the intercept in column 1 of smoothed_state and the first regressor in column 2.

Random.seed!(7)
T = 200
x = randn(T)
β = cumsum(0.05 .* randn(T)) .+ 1.0
y = 0.5 .+ β .* x .+ 0.3 .* randn(T)
tvp = estimate_tvp_reg(y, reshape(x, :, 1))
β_hat = tvp.smoothed_state[:, 2]           # column 2 = slope (column 1 = intercept)
round(cor(β_hat, β), digits=3)
0.951

The smoothed slope path correlates 0.951 with the simulated $\beta_t$, so the filter recovers the drift in the coefficient rather than averaging it away. The estimated hyper-parameters are the irregular variance $\hat\sigma^2_\varepsilon = 0.078$ (the data were generated with $0.3^2 = 0.09$) and the per-coefficient random-walk variances $\hat\sigma^2_\eta = (7 \times 10^{-5},\; 0.0028)$ against the true $(0,\; 0.0025)$: the intercept's variance is driven to the boundary, correctly, because the intercept is constant. Larger $\sigma^2_\eta$ lets a coefficient move more freely over the sample.

Custom systems

For an arbitrary model, pass a builder closure build(θ) -> NamedTuple(Z, H, T, Q, ...) and a starting vector to estimate_statespace. The builder is responsible for the positivity of variance blocks (typically by exponentiating log-variance parameters). Here is an AR(1)-plus-noise unobserved-components model:

Random.seed!(11)
T = 300
φ = 0.7
α = zeros(T)
for t in 2:T; α[t] = φ * α[t-1] + randn(); end
z = α .+ 0.5 .* randn(T)
build = θ -> (Z = reshape([1.0], 1, 1), H = reshape([exp(θ[3])], 1, 1),
              T = reshape([tanh(θ[1])], 1, 1), Q = reshape([exp(θ[2])], 1, 1))
m_ar = estimate_statespace(build, [0.3, 0.0, 0.0], z;
                           param_names = ["atanh(φ)", "logσ²_η", "logσ²_ε"])
round(tanh(m_ar.theta[1]), digits=2)   # recovered persistence φ
0.75

The builder returns $\hat\varphi = 0.75$ against the simulated 0.7, with $\hat\sigma^2_\eta = 0.84$ and $\hat\sigma^2_\varepsilon = 0.29$ against the true 1.0 and 0.25 — the usual trade-off in an unobserved-components model, where a slightly overstated persistence buys a slightly understated state variance. theta holds the raw $\hat\theta$ here, not natural variances, because the default theta_transform=identity applies; the wrappers pass exp instead, which is why their theta is directly readable as variances.

A fixed-matrix StateSpaceModel(Z, H, T, Q; G=nothing, ...) builds an unfitted spec that estimate_statespace(ss, y) filters and smooths without optimization; parametric builders also accept G in the returned NamedTuple.

Steady-State Kalman Filter

For time-invariant observable systems, steady_state_kalman computes the asymptotic one-step-ahead predicted covariance, filtered covariance, Kalman gain, innovation covariance, and closed-loop eigenvalues without iterating over a sample. Following Anderson & Moore (1979, §5.5) and Durbin & Koopman (2012, §4.11), the asymptotic predicted covariance solves the discrete-time algebraic Riccati equation (DARE):

\[\begin{aligned} P_\text{pred} &= T \, P_\text{pred} \, T' + R Q R' - \bigl(T P_\text{pred} Z' + R G\bigr) \, F_\text{innov}^{-1} \, \bigl(T P_\text{pred} Z' + R G\bigr)' \\ F_\text{innov} &= Z \, P_\text{pred} \, Z' + H, \qquad K_\text{gain} = P_\text{pred} \, Z' \, F_\text{innov}^{-1} \end{aligned}\]

where:

  • $P_\text{pred}$ is the $n_x \times n_x$ steady-state predicted state covariance
  • $T$ is the $n_x \times n_x$ state transition matrix
  • $R$ is the $n_x \times n_\eta$ state innovation selection matrix
  • $Q$ is the $n_\eta \times n_\eta$ state disturbance covariance
  • $Z$ is the $n_\text{obs} \times n_x$ observation matrix
  • $G$ is the $n_\eta \times n_\text{obs}$ cross-covariance $\operatorname{Cov}(\eta_t, \varepsilon_t)$ (zero when G === nothing)
  • $H$ is the $n_\text{obs} \times n_\text{obs}$ observation noise covariance
  • $F_\text{innov}$ is the $n_\text{obs} \times n_\text{obs}$ steady-state innovation covariance
  • $K_\text{gain}$ is the $n_x \times n_\text{obs}$ steady-state filtered Kalman gain
Technical Note

The internal solver computes the $2n_x \times 2n_x$ symplectic pencil generalized real Schur decomposition (Pappas, Laub & Sandell 1980), switches to a Van Dooren (1981) QR-compressed extended pencil when $H$ is singular or zero, falls back to the Structure-Preserving Doubling Algorithm if needed, and applies Newton–Kleinman Lyapunov polishing. Closed-loop eigenvalues use $T - K_\text{dare} Z$ with $K_\text{dare} = (T P_\text{pred} Z' + R G) F_\text{innov}^{-1}$.

ssk = steady_state_kalman(m)
(P_pred = round.(ssk.P_pred, digits=2),
 P_filt = round.(ssk.P_filt, digits=2),
 K_gain = round.(ssk.K_gain, digits=4),
 eigs = round.(abs.(ssk.closed_loop_eigs), digits=4))
(P_pred = [5490.62;;], P_filt = [4027.16;;], K_gain = [0.2665;;], eigs = [0.7335])

On the Nile local-level fit, the steady-state predicted variance equals $5490.62$ and the steady-state filtered variance equals $4027.16$, matching the terminal filtered covariance $m.\text{filtered\_cov}[:, :, \text{end}]$ directly. The steady-state Kalman gain $0.2665$ places roughly one quarter of each new innovation into the level update, while the closed-loop eigenvalue modulus $0.7335$ confirms exponential stability of the filter recursion.

For a system with correlated state and observation disturbances, pass G to StateSpaceModel or directly to steady_state_kalman(Z, H, Tt, RQR; G=G):

Z_c = [1.0 0.0]; H_c = reshape([0.5], 1, 1)
T_c = [0.8 0.1; 0.0 0.5]; Q_c = [0.6 0.0; 0.0 0.3]
G_c = reshape([0.3, 0.1], 2, 1)
ssk_c = steady_state_kalman(Z_c, H_c, T_c, Q_c; G=G_c)
round.(ssk_c.K_gain, digits=4)
2×1 Matrix{Float64}:
  0.4635
 -0.0558

With positive cross-covariance $G_c = [0.3, 0.1]'$, the steady-state filtered gain shifts to $[0.4635, -0.0558]'$ and both closed-loop eigenvalues remain inside the unit circle ($0.1346$ and $0.4783$).

Steady-State LQG Rational Inattention

Sims (2003) models an agent tracking an evolving linear-Gaussian state who chooses an endogenous signal distribution subject to a Shannon mutual-information constraint. In steady state, the joint tracking and filtering problem is a Linear-Quadratic-Gaussian (LQG) Rational Inattention problem (Sims 2003; Afrouzi & Yang 2021):

\[\min_{\{\Sigma_{\text{post}}, Y\}} \operatorname{tr}\!\left(\Omega_{\text{benefit}} \, \Sigma_{\text{post}}\right) + \omega \cdot \frac{1}{2}\log\det\!\left(\Sigma_{\text{prior}} \Sigma_{\text{post}}^{-1}\right) \qquad \text{s.t.} \qquad 0 \preceq \Sigma_{\text{post}} \preceq \Sigma_{\text{prior}}, \quad \Sigma_{\text{prior}} = A \Sigma_{\text{post}} A' + B B'\]

where:

  • $(A, B)$ govern state transitions $x_{t+1} = A x_t + B \varepsilon_{t+1}$ with tracking target matrix $H$ and quadratic action weight $M$ (\Omega_{\text{benefit}} derived from H, M, discount $\beta$, and feedback B_u),
  • $\omega > 0$ is the shadow cost per nat of mutual information (or supplied indirectly via Shannon channel capacity capacity in :bits or :nats),
  • $\Sigma_{\text{prior}}$ and $\Sigma_{\text{post}}$ are the steady-state prior and posterior error covariance matrices linked by a water-filling eigenvalue floor,
  • $Y$ is the low-rank endogenous signal extraction matrix giving steady-state Kalman gain $K_{\text{gain}}$.

RationalInattentionProblem specifies the LQG tracking problem and solve_ri_kalman returns a SimsKalmanSolution using either inverse-free whitened water-filling (method=:water_filling) or a Woodbury log-det barrier L-BFGS solver (method=:logdet_barrier).

prob_ri = RationalInattentionProblem([0.85 0.1; 0.0 0.6], [0.5 0.0; 0.0 0.4],
                                     [1.0 0.0; 0.0 0.5]; beta=0.99, capacity=1.0, units=:bits)
ri_sol = solve_ri_kalman(prob_ri)
report(ri_sol)
(capacity_bits = round(ri_sol.capacity_bits, digits=4),
 signal_rank = ri_sol.signal_rank,
 loss = round(ri_sol.expected_loss, digits=4))
(capacity_bits = 1.0, signal_rank = 2, loss = 0.1392)

Dynamic Transition Path and Joint State-Space Dynamics

When initial uncertainty $\Sigma_{1|0} = \Sigma_{\text{init}}$ sits away from the steady state (for instance after a regime shift or uncertainty shock), solve_ri_transition solves Afrouzi & Yang's (2021) dynamic forward–backward Information Euler system anchored at the terminal steady state, returning DynamicRISolution. From the steady-state SimsKalmanSolution, to_statespace and to_abcd build the joint $2n$-dimensional state-space law of motion over true states $x_t$ and rational expectations $\hat{x}_{t|t}$ driven by orthogonal fundamental shocks $w_t$ and endogenous processing noise $\tilde{\psi}_t$, while irf, fevd, and simulate compute sluggish inertial action responses and the processing-noise variance share (Luo 2008).

dyn_ri = solve_ri_transition(prob_ri, [0.8 0.0; 0.0 0.5]; T_path=40)
report(dyn_ri)
irf_ri = irf(ri_sol, 16; observables=:actions)
fevd_ri = fevd(ri_sol, 16; observables=:actions)
sim_ri = simulate(ri_sol, 120; seed=42)
m_shocks = size(prob_ri.B, 2)
(init_bits = round(dyn_ri.capacity_bits_path[1]; digits=4),
 term_bits = round(dyn_ri.capacity_bits_path[end]; digits=4),
 ri_noise_share_h1 = round(sum(fevd_ri.proportions[1, (m_shocks + 1):end, 1]); digits=4),
 sim_T = size(sim_ri.actions, 1))
(init_bits = 1.0, term_bits = 1.0, ri_noise_share_h1 = 0.294, sim_T = 120)

Empirical Estimation and Coibion–Gorodnichenko Diagnostics

Given observed actions, states, or noisy survey aggregates, estimate_ri estimates Shannon channel capacity $\kappa$ (or shadow cost $\omega$) jointly with structural state-space parameters via state-space MLE (method=:mle, returning Hessian or sandwich standard errors and an exact FIRE likelihood-ratio test) or adaptive RWMH (method=:bayes), returning an RIEstimationResult. To test rational-inattention information rigidity directly from forecast errors and revisions (Coibion & Gorodnichenko 2015), implied_cg_slope derives the population Kalman predictability slope and coibion_gorodnichenko_test runs the Newey–West HAC regression returning CGTestResult:

\[y_{t+h} - \bar{F}_t y_{t+h} = \alpha + \beta_{\text{CG}} \bigl(\bar{F}_t y_{t+h} - \bar{F}_{t-1} y_{t+h}\bigr) + \varepsilon_{t+h}, \qquad \beta_{\text{CG}}^{\text{AR(1)}} = \frac{1 - G}{G}\]

where:

  • $y_{t+h} - \bar{F}_t y_{t+h}$ is the consensus forecast error at horizon $h$,
  • $\bar{F}_t y_{t+h} - \bar{F}_{t-1} y_{t+h}$ is the consensus forecast revision,
  • $G = 1 - 2^{-2\kappa}$ is the scalar AR(1) steady-state Kalman gain implied by Shannon capacity $\kappa$ bits.
using Random
prob_1d = RationalInattentionProblem(0.8, 1.0, 1.0; capacity=0.75)
sol_1d = solve_ri_kalman(prob_1d)
sim_1d = simulate(sol_1d, 100; burn=40, seed=42)
ri_est = estimate_ri(prob_1d, hcat(sim_1d.states, sim_1d.actions); method=:mle)
cg_pop = implied_cg_slope(sol_1d)
rng_cg = Random.Xoshiro(920)
rev_cg = randn(rng_cg, 80)
fe_cg = cg_pop .* rev_cg .+ 0.3 .* randn(rng_cg, 80)
cg_res = coibion_gorodnichenko_test(fe_cg .+ rev_cg, rev_cg, zeros(80))
report(ri_est)
(kappa_hat = round(ri_est.capacity; digits=3),
 cg_implied = round(cg_pop; digits=3),
 cg_ols = round(cg_res.beta_cg; digits=3))
(kappa_hat = 0.696, cg_implied = 0.547, cg_ols = 0.55)

Multi-Block Price-Setting Attention Allocation

Maćkowiak & Wiederholt (2009, 2015) endogenize how a decision maker splits scarce total attention $\kappa_{\text{total}} = \kappa_{\text{agg}} + \kappa_{\text{idio}}$ across independent aggregate and idiosyncratic fundamentals under strategic complementarity $r \in [0, 1)$:

\[\max_{\kappa_{\text{agg}},\, \kappa_{\text{idio}} \ge 0} \; -L_{\text{agg}}(\kappa_{\text{agg}}) - L_{\text{idio}}(\kappa_{\text{idio}}) \qquad \text{s.t.} \qquad \kappa_{\text{agg}} + \kappa_{\text{idio}} \le \kappa_{\text{total}}\]

where:

  • $L_{\text{agg}}(\kappa_{\text{agg}})$ and $L_{\text{idio}}(\kappa_{\text{idio}})$ are block LQG tracking losses solved to common marginal shadow cost $\omega$,
  • $r \in [0, 1)$ amplifies aggregate sluggishness via the fixed-point best-response pricing multiplier $p_t = (1 - r) E_t[\Delta_t + \dots]$.

solve_mw_attention computes exact water-filling capacity shares and equilibrium impulse responses in MWAttentionResult.

agg_blk = (; rho=0.88, sigma=0.02, weight=1.0, beta=0.99)
idio_blk = (; rho=0.75, sigma=0.10, weight=1.0, beta=0.99)
mw_res = solve_mw_attention(agg_blk, idio_blk; capacity=2.0, strategic_complementarity=0.7, horizon=20)
report(mw_res)
(kappa_agg = round(mw_res.kappa_agg; digits=3), kappa_idio = round(mw_res.kappa_idio; digits=3))
(kappa_agg = 0.079, kappa_idio = 1.921)

Complete Example

Estimate the Nile local level, inspect residual diagnostics, and forecast five years ahead:

m = local_level(nile)

# One-step standardized residuals should be white noise on a well-specified model.
r = vec(m.std_residuals); r = r[.!isnan.(r)]
lb = ljung_box_test(r; lags=10)
round(lb.pvalue, digits=3)
0.168

The Ljung-Box statistic on the standardized one-step residuals gives $p = 0.168$, so the residuals are indistinguishable from white noise at any conventional level and the local level is an adequate description of the series.

The predictive standard error grows from 143.5 to 162.7 over five years: each extra step adds exactly one level variance $\hat\sigma^2_\eta = 1463.5$ to the forecast variance, so the bands widen as $\sqrt{\text{Var}(y_{T+1}) + (h-1)\,\hat\sigma^2_\eta}$ — the square-root-of-time widening of an integrated process, not the flattening of a stationary one.

Common Pitfalls

  1. Log-likelihood is not directly comparable to exact-diffuse software. The large-$\kappa$ prior keeps a $-\tfrac12\log\kappa$ offset for the diffuse observation, whereas exact-diffuse implementations (KFAS, statsmodels with initialization='diffuse') drop it. The MLE variance estimates and filtered states still match to within a fraction of a percent.
  2. Variance components hitting zero. When a variance is truly zero (e.g. a fixed intercept in a TVP regression), its log-parameter drifts to $-\infty$ and the optimizer reports converged=false even though the fit is correct. Check the recovered paths, not just the flag.
  3. Smoothed states do not exactly satisfy the observation equation. Use a relaxed tolerance if you compare $Z\,\alpha_{t|T} + d$ against $y_t$.
  4. Custom builders must keep H and Q positive. Parameterize variances as exp(θ) and persistences as tanh(θ); a raw clamp flattens the gradient at the boundary and stalls LBFGS.
